# Recovery and Health Coach — Internal Evidence Base

Compact operational notes from local summaries. Read the linked local summary for context before relying on a specific finding. These notes are internal decision support, not clinical guidance or proof of full-text review. Keep all keys and source details out of user-facing answers.

## Sleep and recovery — `walsh2021sleep`

Summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/walsh2021sleep/walsh2021sleep-resume.md`.

- Narrative review/consensus, focused predominantly on elite athletes with heterogeneous methods and incomplete athlete-specific evidence.
- The consensus describes 7–9 hours as a healthy-adult range and 8–10 hours for teenagers, while emphasizing individual sleep needs and circumstances; do not impose a single athlete target or treat it as a threshold.
- Sleep education and sleep extension findings are context-specific. Generic “sleep more” advice can be unsuitable, including for insomnia. Persistent or substantial sleep concerns warrant appropriate professional review.
- Consumer sleep devices may misestimate sleep and can increase anxiety or preoccupation; use self-report and trends cautiously.
- The summary identifies no operational recovery formula.

## Low energy availability and RED-S — `mountjoy2023reds`, historical context `mountjoy2018reds`

Summaries: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/mountjoy2023reds/mountjoy2023reds-resume.md` and `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/mountjoy2018reds/mountjoy2018reds-resume.md`.

- The 2023 IOC consensus frames REDs as a multifactorial clinical assessment, distinguishes transient/adaptable from problematic low energy availability, and rejects a universal energy-availability diagnostic cutoff.
- The frequently repeated 30 kcal/kg FFM/day arose from limited short-term laboratory research in small samples of sedentary females; it is not a universal diagnostic threshold. The proposed male ranges are emerging, not validated cutoffs. Measurement inputs are difficult and imprecise.
- Potential signs can affect multiple systems and have other causes. Do not diagnose from symptoms, a checklist, consumer-scale values, weight/shape, or arithmetic. Do not calculate energy availability for individual diagnosis, risk classification, reassurance, or intake prescription.
- The consensus clinical process requires qualified expert assessment; recommend an appropriately qualified physician and sports dietitian when concerns warrant it. Do not try to treat suspected REDs through extra exercise, restriction, or supplements.
- The 2018 summary's 45 and 30 kcal/kg FFM/day values are historical context from controlled female laboratory work, not individual cutoffs; the later consensus rejects threshold-based diagnosis.

## Injury prevention — `lauersen2014prevention`

Summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/lauersen2014prevention/lauersen2014prevention-resume.md`.

- Meta-analysis of 25 prevention RCTs in initially uninjured participants across varied ages, sports, interventions, and follow-up; pooled effects were heterogeneous and had a small-study effect.
- Strength, proprioception, and multicomponent programs showed pooled estimates favoring fewer injuries; the review did not identify one standard protocol or dose. Stretching alone did not show a statistically supported preventive effect.
- Primary prevention findings are not treatment/rehab evidence, not an individualized promise, and not a reason to add a corrective exercise block.

## Load monitoring and tolerance — `gabbett2016paradox`

Summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/gabbett2016paradox/gabbett2016paradox-resume.md`.

- Narrative/conceptual review based mainly on elite team sports, especially rugby league. Both excessive/rapidly changed exposure and insufficient preparation may be problematic; interpret external work alongside individual response and context.
- Session RPE × session duration is optional within-person bookkeeping, not a standardized dose or injury-risk score.
- ACWR zones and the <10% weekly-change suggestion are context-bound team-sport heuristics, partly based on unpublished observations; do not calculate/use them to classify health, injury risk, or safe progression.

## Running injury etiology — `bertelsen2017etiology`

Summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/bertelsen2017etiology/bertelsen2017etiology-resume.md`.

- Conceptual model: tissue-specific capacity, accumulated loading, recovery, and contextual factors can interact. It is not a validated prediction model and provides no universal threshold.
- Distance, time, and stride count are exposure proxies, not direct tissue-load measures. Tissue-specific load/capacity cannot be calculated with a field-ready formula from supplied data.

## Endurance injury observations — `johnston2018enduranceinjury`

Summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/johnston2018enduranceinjury/johnston2018enduranceinjury-resume.md`.

- Relevant to running-context discussions; its observational endurance cohorts had varied populations and injury/load definitions and mixed results. Do not infer causal or individual risk thresholds from distance, frequency, BMI, or history.

## Tapering — `bosquet2007tapering`

Summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/bosquet2007tapering/bosquet2007tapering-resume.md`.

- The local summary describes a meta-analysis in well-trained endurance athletes (the resume identifies the analyzed article as a 2023 synthesis despite the local key's 2007 label). Its taper subgroup ranges are not a universal prescription and apply to pre-event performance, not general recovery or a scheduled deload for every recreational trainee.
- Do not create, time, or quantify a taper. Refer competition-specific taper choices to the running/endurance specialist.

## Cross-cutting limits

- Recovery includes sleep, nutrition/energy, tolerable load, spacing, life stress, illness, and response over time; no supplied single measure captures it.
- Separate source findings, interpretation, and practical option. Do not turn a group average, athlete consensus, heuristic, or pooled prevention effect into a personal target or promise.
- Symptoms take priority over body metrics. Stop/pause and refer when scope or severity requires; do not assess return-to-sport readiness.
