# Summary — ramoscampo2024split

## Central idea

This systematic review and meta-analysis compared split and full-body resistance-training routines for strength and muscle growth in healthy adults. When weekly training volume was equated, the pooled estimates did not show a meaningful difference between routine distributions; the findings support choosing a format that fits an individual’s circumstances rather than treating either split or full-body training as inherently superior.

## Context and scope

The PRISMA-based, preregistered review searched PubMed-Medline and Web of Science through June 23, 2023. It included 14 controlled interventions (392 participants; 194 in split and 198 in full-body groups), with study participants aged 18 to under 40 years. Two studies enrolled women, 11 enrolled men, and one enrolled both; most participants were young adults, and experience ranged from untrained to trained. Programs lasted 4–12 weeks. The comparisons concerned how exercises were distributed across sessions, generally with weekly training volume matched; routine definitions and frequency distributions varied among studies. Outcomes included bench-press and lower-limb 1RM, muscle cross-sectional area, and lean body mass. Ten studies were judged low risk of bias and four had some concerns under the authors’ RoB2 assessment.

## Relevant findings

- **Review finding—strength:** Split versus full-body training produced similar changes in bench-press 1RM (full-body minus split mean difference 1.19 kg, 95% CI −1.28 to 3.65; *p* = 0.34) and lower-limb 1RM (2.47 kg, 95% CI −2.11 to 7.05; *p* = 0.29). Statistical heterogeneity was low (*I*² = 0% for each outcome).
- **Review finding—muscle growth:** The pooled estimates did not indicate a difference in elbow-extensor cross-sectional area (MD 0.30, 95% CI −2.65 to 3.24; *p* = 0.84), elbow-flexor cross-sectional area (MD 0.17, 95% CI −2.54 to 2.88; *p* = 0.91), or vastus-lateralis cross-sectional area (MD −0.08, 95% CI −1.82 to 1.66; *p* = 0.93). The paper does not clearly state the measurement units for these pooled raw mean differences in its narrative. Lean body mass also showed no difference. The abstract reports a raw MD of −0.07 (95% CI −1.59 to 1.44), whereas the detailed results report an SMD of 0.002 (95% CI −0.28 to 0.31; *p* = 0.91; *I*² = 0%). These differently scaled estimates are not reconciled in the paper; both are consistent with no detected difference.
- **Authors’ practical conclusion:** With volume equated, people may select a split or full-body organization according to preference and schedule. This does not establish equivalence for programs with unequal volume, longer time frames, or every training population.
- Interpretation is limited by the small number of studies involving women and highly trained participants, short intervention durations, differences in how routine organization and frequency were implemented, and variation in muscle-size measurement. Some body-composition measures, such as bioelectrical impedance, are less direct and may be influenced by hydration.
