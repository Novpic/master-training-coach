# Summary — schumann2022compatibility

## Central idea

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated whether adding aerobic training to strength training reduces gains in maximal strength, explosive strength, or muscle hypertrophy. On average, maximal strength and hypertrophy were not detectably compromised, while explosive-strength gains were smaller with concurrent training, particularly when both modes were performed close together in the same session.

## Context and scope

The review included 43 supervised training studies involving 1,090 healthy adults of any sex or age. Eligible studies compared concurrent aerobic-plus-strength training lasting at least 4 weeks with the same strength-training prescription alone. The pooled analyses included 37 studies for maximal strength, 18 for explosive strength, and 15 for hypertrophy. Explosive-strength outcomes included jump tests, rate of force development, or dynamic power; hypertrophy required objective muscle-size measures. Study quality was modest overall (mean PEDro score 4.3/10). Aerobic-training intensity could not be tested as a moderator because reporting was inconsistent.

## Relevant findings

- **Meta-analysis findings:** Concurrent training showed no statistically detectable average interference for maximal strength (SMD −0.06, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.09) or muscle hypertrophy (SMD −0.01, 95% CI −0.16 to 0.18). These pooled estimates do not mean every individual or program responds identically.
- **Explosive strength:** The pooled estimate favored strength training alone (SMD −0.28, 95% CI −0.48 to −0.08). A subgroup effect was observed when aerobic and strength work were performed within the same session (defined as no more than 20 minutes apart; SMD −0.31, 95% CI −0.62 to −0.01). The subgroup with sessions separated by at least 3 hours did not show a statistically significant difference. The review found no order-specific effect.
- The authors did not detect moderator differences by cycling versus running, lower versus higher concurrent-training frequency, untrained versus active status, or mean age category. These null subgroup results do not establish equivalence; subgroup precision and intervention reporting were limited.
- **Author recommendation:** People prioritizing explosive strength may consider separating aerobic and strength sessions; the subgroup evidence supports this as a cautious programming option, not a universal timing rule.
