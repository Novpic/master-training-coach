# Summary — mountjoy2023reds

## Central idea

The IOC's 2023 consensus defines REDs as impaired physiological and/or psychological functioning in female and male athletes caused by problematic—prolonged and/or severe—low energy availability (LEA). It distinguishes potentially benign, transient “adaptable LEA” from “problematic LEA,” and replaces reliance on universal quantitative cutoffs with an individualized, multi-factor assessment framework.

## Context and scope

This is a consensus statement synthesizing research and expert judgment, not a new intervention study. The authors report approximately 178 original REDs/LEA studies since the 2018 update, involving about 23,822 participants; approximately 80% were female and about 62% of studies were cross-sectional. The statement used expert working groups, confidential Delphi voting, and an in-person consensus process. Comparisons remain difficult because diagnosis and measurement are not standardized and EA is difficult to estimate reliably.

## Relevant findings

- The updated health and performance models place LEA exposure on a continuum and consider its magnitude, duration, frequency, origin, and individual or environmental moderators. The health model depicts possible effects across reproductive, bone, metabolic, immune, gastrointestinal, cardiovascular, musculoskeletal, and mental-health systems. These are conceptual, evidence-informed outcomes—not diagnostic proof—and each sign or symptom can have other causes.
- EA is the dietary energy remaining after exercise expenditure, expressed relative to fat-free mass (FFM) per day. This can inform research or assessment but is not, by itself, a REDs diagnosis.
- The statement rejects a single clinical EA threshold. The often-cited **30 kcal/kg FFM/day** value originated in short-term laboratory research in small samples of habitually sedentary females and was intended as a guide, not a diagnostic endpoint. The value is debated and should not be applied universally. A lower **~9–25 kcal/kg FFM/day** range is discussed as emerging evidence for possible REDs-related symptoms in men, not as a validated threshold; male evidence remains limited. REDs prevalence in para-athletes is unknown, and disability-specific factors complicate assessment.
- Low carbohydrate availability may add to or magnify some LEA-related biomarker effects. The review describes short controlled interventions, including a 3-day study in young females and an approximately 3.5-week low-carbohydrate/high-fat intervention in elite endurance athletes. These findings concern short-term biomarkers and do not establish long-term clinical outcomes.
- **Author-recommended clinical process:** the IOC REDs CAT2 has three steps: population-specific screening questionnaires and/or clinical interviews; accumulated severity/risk assessment and stratification; and expert-physician diagnosis and treatment, ideally with a multidisciplinary team. The tool is not a stand-alone diagnosis. Differential diagnosis is required, and participation guidance should not replace professional assessment.
- **Author recommendations on prevention and safety:** restore adequate EA through individualized dietary and exercise changes with appropriate macro- and micronutrient intake and multidisciplinary support. Body-composition assessment should be restricted to medical purposes for athletes under 18, with exceptional cases requiring careful team consensus and guardian consent. The accompanying developmental framework emphasizes growth and health over performance-driven body-composition targets in developing athletes.

## Key formulas

$$
EA = \frac{EI - EEE}{FFM}
$$

`EI` is dietary energy intake and `EEE` is exercise energy expenditure (kcal/day); `FFM` is fat-free mass (kg). EA is expressed as kcal/kg FFM/day.
