# Summary — schoenfeld2016rest

## Central idea

This randomized training trial compared 1-minute with 3-minute interset rests in young resistance-trained men over 8 weeks. In this specific program, 3-minute rests produced greater gains in 1RM squat and bench press and greater anterior-thigh muscle-thickness growth; upper-body endurance improved in both groups without a significant between-group difference. The study does not establish a universal rest prescription or isolate whether the results arose from rest duration itself or from the greater training work that longer rests may permit.

## Context and scope

Twenty-three men aged 18–35 were recruited; 21 completed (10 in the 3-minute group and 11 in the 1-minute group). Participants had at least 6 months of consistent lifting and a back-squat/body-mass ratio of at least 1.0. They were pair-matched by baseline squat strength and randomly assigned. For 8 weeks, both groups performed three nonconsecutive-day, full-body sessions weekly: 3 sets of 8–12RM for 7 exercises, with sets taken to momentary concentric failure, controlled repetitions, and attempts to increase loads while staying in the target range. Rest between sets was 1 or 3 minutes; other planned training variables were held constant. Outcomes were 1RM bench press and squat, repetitions to failure at 50% baseline bench-press 1RM, and ultrasound muscle thickness at specified arm and thigh sites.

## Relevant findings

- **Reported trial findings:** The 3-minute group had significantly greater 8-week increases in 1RM squat (**15.2% vs. 7.6%**) and bench press (**12.7% vs. 4.1%**, with the shorter-rest group's within-group change only a statistical trend). Anterior quadriceps thickness increased **13.3% vs. 6.9%**, significantly favoring 3-minute rests. Both groups increased vastus-lateralis thickness (**11.5% vs. 10.0%**) without a significant between-group difference. Elbow-flexor thickness did not differ significantly between groups; the between-group triceps result favored longer rest only as a trend (**p = 0.06**). These are site-specific ultrasound measures, not whole-muscle volume estimates.
- **Reported trial findings—endurance and training work:** Bench-press repetitions at 50% of baseline 1RM increased in both groups (**23.2%** with 3-minute rests and **13.0%** with 1-minute rests), with no significant between-group difference. The group-level association between percentage change in bench-press 1RM and repetitions was **r = 0.75**; this correlation does not establish causation. The 3-minute group accumulated a numerically greater total load over 8 weeks, but the difference was not statistically significant, and load tracking covered only three barbell exercises. The study could not determine whether greater training volume mediated the adaptation differences.
- **Authors' practical interpretation:** Considering this trial alongside earlier studies, the authors suggested at least about **2 minutes of rest** when maximizing muscle-size gains is the goal. This is a cautious interpretation rather than a direct comparison of every rest duration: the trial tested 1 vs. 3 minutes, and the authors noted that the longer-rest sessions took more than twice as long. Rest needs may therefore involve a training-time trade-off.
- **Limitations:** The intervention lasted only 8 weeks and involved a small sample of young trained men. Diet was not controlled. Machine-based exercise loads could not be reliably included in volume-load analysis, and muscle thickness was measured only at mid-muscle sites. The results should not be generalized to women, beginners, older adults, other endurance outcomes, or different training designs without further evidence.

## Key formulas

The study's tracked volume load was calculated as:

$$
\text{Volume load} = \text{load} \times \text{repetitions} \times \text{sets}
$$

Load is recorded in kg; repetitions and sets are counts. In this paper it was assessed for the last weekly session of three barbell exercises and should not be mistaken for a complete measure of all training stimulus.
