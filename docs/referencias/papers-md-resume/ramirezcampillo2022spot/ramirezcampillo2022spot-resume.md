# Summary — ramirezcampillo2022spot

## Central idea

This systematic review and meta-analysis evaluated whether voluntarily training one limb reduces the adjacent non-intramuscular fat depot more than leaving the contralateral limb untrained. Across the included interventions, the pooled result did not support exercise-induced spot reduction as the paper defines it.

## Context and scope

The authors searched PubMed, Web of Science, and Scopus through August 2021 and included 13 controlled studies (1,158 participants, ages 14–71) with healthy participants of varied fitness and training backgrounds. Each study compared localized training of one limb with the participant’s contralateral, untrained limb and assessed pre-to-post change in a fat-related outcome. Interventions lasted 2–20 weeks; resistance-training loads ranged from 10% to 90% of 1RM, while the cycling intervention used approximately 40% of peak oxygen consumption. The review used random-effects meta-analysis of 37 comparisons. The authors’ definition concerns non-intramuscular adipose tissue, such as subcutaneous fat; it does not rule on every possible definition of regional fat change.

## Relevant findings

- **Review finding:** The pooled effect was trivial and not statistically distinguishable from no trained-versus-control limb difference (Hedges’ *g* = −0.03, 95% CI −0.10 to 0.05; *p* = 0.508; *I*² = 24.3%). Seventeen comparisons favored the trained limb and 20 the untrained limb, with no consistent localized reduction overall. The forest plot depicts this near-zero pooled estimate, not a universal response for every person or measurement method.
- The authors note that acute localized lipolysis reported in some cross-sectional research is not itself evidence of a lasting reduction in an adipose depot. They distinguish that acute response from the chronic pre-to-post outcome assessed by the review.
- **Interpretation and scope:** These results do not establish that exercise cannot reduce total body fat or that regional body composition is identical across all populations and interventions. The included trials were voluntary unilateral-limb interventions of limited duration; longer interventions and non-voluntary activation strategies were not assessed.
- Important limitations include possible activation of the control limb, variable fat-measurement methods, and incomplete reporting of adverse effects. Dietary intake was not required to be controlled, although a contralateral-limb comparison helps control many participant-level factors.
