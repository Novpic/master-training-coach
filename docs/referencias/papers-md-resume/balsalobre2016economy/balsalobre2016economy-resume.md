# Summary — balsalobre2016economy

## Central idea

This systematic review and meta-analysis examined whether strength-training programs improve running economy in highly trained middle- and long-distance runners. Across five controlled trials, the pooled result favored strength training, although the evidence base was small and heterogeneous, and one study contributed unusually large effects.

## Context and scope

The authors searched four databases through September 2015 and included peer-reviewed controlled trials of competitive middle- or long-distance runners with maximal oxygen uptake (VO₂max) above 60 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹, interventions longer than 4 weeks, and pre/post running-economy (RE) measures. Five studies (93 runners: 78 men and 15 women, ages 17.3–29.8 years) met criteria; three were randomized and two used non-randomized matching. Programs lasted 8–12 weeks and were compared with control training. Study quality was modest (mean PEDro score 5.4; range 5–6). The authors used a random-effects model of standardized mean differences (SMDs). This is a synthesis of five trials, not a single intervention, and its findings do not directly generalize to recreational runners or other athlete groups.

## Relevant findings

- **Review finding:** Mean RE changed by −2.32 ± 2.07 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ in strength-training groups and +0.57 ± 2.48 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ in controls. The detailed results report a significant large pooled effect, SMD **−1.43** (95% CI **−2.23 to −0.64**; p < 0.001). The abstract instead reports **−1.42** (95% CI **−2.23 to −0.60**); the paper does not reconcile this small abstract/body discrepancy. Heterogeneity was significant (I² = 61%, p = 0.03). Removing the Paavolainen study eliminated detected heterogeneity and retained a significant effect, SMD −1.06 (95% CI −1.56 to −0.56; p < 0.001).
- **Programs represented:** Four of five trials used 40–70% 1RM resistance work, generally alongside plyometrics and/or short sprints; the remaining trial used half-squats at 85% 1RM. The review describes 2–4 lower-body resistance exercises, up to 200 jumps and/or 5–10 sprints of 20–150 m, usually 2–3 sessions per week for 8–12 weeks. No included program trained repetitions to failure. The single heavy-load protocol used four sets of four half-squats, about 15 minutes per session.
- **Authors’ practical recommendation:** For highly trained distance runners, consider 2–4 resistance exercises at 40–70% 1RM without reaching failure, with plyometrics, 2–3 times weekly for 8–12 weeks, while balancing concurrent endurance work. This is a recommendation inferred from a few small trials, not a universally established prescription. The source reports runners also performed 6–9 endurance sessions weekly.
- **Limitations:** The small sample of trials, modest trial-quality scores and heterogeneity constrain confidence and transferability. One trial had a substantially larger effect than the others without a clear explanation. The analysis concerns RE; it does not establish a guaranteed improvement in race performance.
