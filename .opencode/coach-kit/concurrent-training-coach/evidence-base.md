# Concurrent Training Evidence Base

Internal decision notes for `concurrent-training-coach`. This is a compact synthesis of the repository's Markdown paper summaries, not an independent full-text review. Use the source-summary paths below to inspect the detailed scope and limitations when needed. Do not include these references or citations in user-facing training plans.

## Compatibility of aerobic and resistance training — `schumann2022compatibility`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/schumann2022compatibility/schumann2022compatibility-resume.md`.

- Systematic review/meta-analysis of 43 supervised studies (1,090 healthy adults); concurrent aerobic-plus-strength programs of at least four weeks were compared with the same strength prescription alone.
- No statistically detectable average interference for maximal strength (SMD −0.06, 95% CI −0.20 to 0.09) or whole-muscle hypertrophy (SMD −0.01, 95% CI −0.16 to 0.18). These estimates are not evidence that every individual/program responds identically.
- Explosive-strength gains were smaller on average in concurrent programs (SMD −0.28, 95% CI −0.48 to −0.08). A subgroup signal occurred when modalities were performed within the same session (defined as ≤20 minutes apart); a separated-session subgroup did not show a statistically significant difference. No order-specific effect was detected.
- Study quality was modest and aerobic-intensity reporting was inconsistent. Treat separation as a practical option for people prioritizing explosive strength, not a guaranteed or universal interval.

## Fiber hypertrophy — `lundberg2022fiber`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/lundberg2022fiber/lundberg2022fiber-resume.md`.

- Systematic review/meta-analysis of 15 studies and 300 healthy adults; supervised concurrent training was compared with matched strength-only training for muscle-fiber size.
- Overall fiber hypertrophy showed a small borderline attenuation (SMD −0.23, 95% CI −0.46 to approximately 0; p=0.050; I²=58.6%). Type I and type II pooled estimates were individually non-significant and imprecise.
- The type-I running subgroup included only three studies (SMD −0.81), and running-vs-cycling subgroup differences were not statistically significant. Do not treat this as evidence that running is universally worse or cycling protects whole-muscle hypertrophy.
- Fiber biopsy outcomes are not interchangeable with whole-muscle size; small samples and measurement variability limit certainty. The review does not yield a specific training prescription.

## Exercise sequence — `eddens2018sequence`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/eddens2018sequence/eddens2018sequence-resume.md`.

- Systematic review/meta-analysis of 10 studies (245 participants) comparing resistance-then-endurance with endurance-then-resistance within sessions separated by no more than 15 minutes.
- Resistance before endurance produced a 6.91 percentage-point greater change in dynamic lower-body strength (95% CI 1.96 to 11.87; p=0.006; I²=66%). Heterogeneity was substantial.
- No clear sequencing effect was detected for lower-body hypertrophy, static strength, maximal aerobic capacity, or body-fat percentage. Lack of significance is not proof that sequences are equivalent; there were too few studies to assess power.
- Practical implication: when sessions must be combined closely and dynamic lower-body strength is a priority, strength-first is a reasonable choice. Do not generalize this into a universal order rule.

## Acute aerobic exercise before strength testing — `markov2022acute`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/markov2022acute/markov2022acute-resume.md`.

- Systematic review/meta-analysis of 15 repeated-measures studies involving 197 trained males aged 18–42; outcomes were measured within 15 minutes after running, cycling, or small-sided soccer.
- Aerobic exercise was followed by lower acute strength on average (SMD 0.79, 95% CI 0.38 to 1.21); pooled power change was not statistically significant and uncertain.
- Subgroup comparisons do not establish intensity- or duration-based rules. The apparent modality contrast is based on few studies and knee-extensor tests; it conflicts with some long-term literature.
- This is acute testing in trained males, not long-term concurrent programming evidence. Do not use it for a sex-specific prescription or claim an order guarantees adaptation.

## Concurrent training in adult females — `mikkonen2024femaleconcurrent`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/mikkonen2024femaleconcurrent/mikkonen2024femaleconcurrent-resume.md`.

- Descriptive systematic review of 14 studies of apparently healthy adult females, primarily reproductive age; studies combined strength with running, cycling, rowing, or cross-country skiing.
- Available reports generally described strength and endurance improvements, but the evidence was sparse, heterogeneous, and low-to-moderate quality. Few studies had single-mode comparators, so the review could not establish or rule out interference.
- Menstrual status and contraceptive use were often not reported or controlled. The review found no basis for menstrual-cycle-phase-based periodization and insufficient evidence for specific female-only adjustments.
- The review's recreational-practice suggestions are cautious interpretations, not universal prescriptions. Do not use sex, cycle phase, or contraceptive status alone to prescribe a different split or workload.

## Concurrent-training moderators — `wilson2012concurrent`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/wilson2012concurrent/wilson2012concurrent-resume.md`.

- Older meta-analysis of 21 primary studies (422 effect sizes); samples and protocols were heterogeneous and mainly recreationally/strength trained.
- Overall summaries found no significant difference in hypertrophy or strength; lower-body power was more susceptible on average. In a running subgroup, lower-body strength and hypertrophy effect sizes were smaller than strength-only comparisons, but these are limited subgroup comparisons.
- Study-level associations linked greater endurance frequency and average session duration with smaller adaptation effect sizes. They do not establish causation, personal dose thresholds, or an individual response.
- Author examples about session duration/frequency are not experimentally established universal cutoffs. Do not enforce them as plan rules or claim cycling eliminates interference.

## Cross-study decision boundaries

- Maximal strength, dynamic strength, explosive power, whole-muscle hypertrophy, and biopsy-measured fiber size are distinct outcomes; never use one as a proxy for all the others.
- There is no validated universal threshold for concurrent frequency, duration, same-day order, or hours between sessions in these summaries.
- Base the plan on stated priority, recent tolerated training, logistics, and monitoring. Explain timing choices as practical scheduling, not as a guarantee.
- Treat research effect sizes as group-level statistics only. Do not calculate or predict a user's likely gains from them.
