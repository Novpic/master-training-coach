# Running Coach Internal Evidence Base

Compact working synthesis of the repository's running-related paper summaries. Use as internal decision support only. This file is not a bibliography and does not establish independent full-text verification. Do not copy its source labels or identifiers into a plan response.

## General exercise planning — `garber2011exercise`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/garber2011exercise/garber2011exercise-resume.md`.

- Professional position stand for apparently healthy adults, synthesizing evidence available through 2010; not a trial and not primarily an advanced-athlete prescription.
- Recommends general cardiorespiratory activity targets for health while emphasizing that lower amounts can benefit previously sedentary adults and that duration, frequency, and intensity should progress gradually toward personal goals.
- Apply as broad health/fitness context, not as a mandatory entry dose, individualized race plan, or proven universal beginner protocol.

## Strength training and running performance — `blagrove2018strength`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/blagrove2018strength/blagrove2018strength-resume.md`.

- Systematic review of 24 studies, mostly 6–14 weeks, involving middle-/long-distance runners with at least six months' running experience. Qualitative synthesis with moderate-quality, heterogeneous trials and inconsistent results.
- Many studies reported improvements in running economy, time trials, or sprint outcomes after strength training; others were null. VO2max and body-composition changes were generally not significant.
- The review's suggested 2–3 strength sessions/week, exercise/set ranges, and timing around hard runs describe studied programs or author recommendations, not a universal beginner dose or proof of injury prevention.
- When useful, frame strength work as a complement to running and accommodate it as a recovery/scheduling constraint. Do not provide a full strength plan unless requested.

## Strength training and running economy — `balsalobre2016economy`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/balsalobre2016economy/balsalobre2016economy-resume.md`.

- Systematic review/meta-analysis of five controlled trials and 93 competitive, highly trained middle-/long-distance runners (VO2max above 60); mostly young adults, interventions 8–12 weeks, modest study quality.
- Pooled result favored strength training for running economy, but heterogeneity was significant, the evidence base was small, and one study had unusually large effects. Running economy is not itself a guaranteed race-time change.
- Do not transfer the point estimate or protocol directly to recreational beginners, promise a performance gain, or claim a single best strength approach.

## Injury etiology framework — `bertelsen2017etiology`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/bertelsen2017etiology/bertelsen2017etiology-resume.md`.

- Conceptual framework, not an intervention, cohort estimate, or validated prediction model: tissue-specific capacity, accumulated structure-specific loading, recovery, and multiple contextual factors may interact.
- Distance/time and stride count are exposure proxies, not direct measurements of tissue load. Surface, terrain, speed, body mass, training/injury history, recovery, and other factors may affect loading/capacity.
- The local formula for cumulative tissue loading is conceptual and lacks a practical universal unit or field measurement method. Never compute it or present a tissue-capacity threshold.
- Supports individualized monitoring and gradual capacity building, not a universal safe progression, diagnostic checklist, or injury-prevention guarantee.

## Training load and endurance-sport injury — `johnston2018enduranceinjury`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/johnston2018enduranceinjury/johnston2018enduranceinjury-resume.md`.

- Systematic review of 12 prospective cohorts across adult running, rowing, and triathlon; populations, load measures, pain/injury definitions, and analyses varied, so no meta-analysis was performed.
- Some studies linked higher distance/volume or prior injury to later injury/pain; other load analyses were null. Previous injury was among the more consistently reported associations, but not universal. BMI was not significantly associated in the studies that assessed it.
- Findings are observational, endurance-sport-wide, and not causal thresholds. Do not use individual cohort cutoffs, weekly frequency, BMI, or old-injury history alone to label a runner high-risk or prescribe a dose.
- A cautious practical implication is to monitor and adjust exposure in light of the runner's own baseline and history; this is not a validated risk score.

## Injury prevention exercise interventions — `lauersen2014prevention`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/lauersen2014prevention/lauersen2014prevention-resume.md`.

- Meta-analysis of 25 randomized sports injury-prevention trials in initially uninjured participants across varied sports, ages, interventions, and follow-up. The pooled estimate was heterogeneous and had a small-study effect.
- Strength, proprioception, and multicomponent programs had pooled estimates favoring lower injury incidence; stretching alone did not show a statistically supported effect. Interventions and populations varied, and subgroup evidence does not define a standard running protocol.
- This is primary prevention, not treatment/rehabilitation and not a guarantee that any runner's injury risk will fall. Do not translate pooled relative risks into an individual promise.

## Load monitoring concepts — `gabbett2016paradox`

Local summary: `docs/referencias/papers-md-resume/gabbett2016paradox/gabbett2016paradox-resume.md`.

- Narrative review and conceptual model based mainly on elite team sports, especially rugby league. It argues both excessive/rapidly changed load and insufficient preparation may be problematic; load should be interpreted with the athlete's response and context.
- The article presents session load as session RPE multiplied by duration. This can be used as simple within-person bookkeeping, not as a standardized physiological dose or injury-risk score.
- Its acute:chronic workload ratio zones and <10% weekly-change suggestion derive largely from team-sport evidence, including unpublished observations. They are not validated universal rules and must not be used to classify running load as safe/unsafe.

## Interpretation boundaries

- Describe what a source studied and its limits internally before applying it. An observed program or author recommendation is not automatically the optimal personal plan.
- Keep running performance, running economy, general health activity, and injury prevention as distinct outcomes.
- Do not infer running fitness, training capacity, injury susceptibility, or an individualized prescription from BIA scale fields, body circumferences, sex/gender, BMI, or body shape.
- The repository's running overview in `docs/03-carrera/03-carrera.md` is a practical topic summary; it is not itself a paper or universal prescription. Use it for local terminology and principles, with the above study boundaries.
